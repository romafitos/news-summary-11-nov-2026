World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. Two days of US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine ended in Abu Dhabi with a large prisoner swap agreed — 1,000 to 1,500 captives each, US envoy Jared Kushner said — but no ceasefire and no deal on territory (Reuters). Kushner and Steve Witkoff fly to Moscow on Thursday, then to Dublin to meet President Zelenskyy. Overnight, Russia hit six large power substations in central Ukraine and a market strike in the Odesa region killed three.

At the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, UN chief Antonio Guterres opened the leaders' summit by urging taxes on windfall oil profits, noting the nine biggest oil firms earned $95 billion in a quarter while the Loss and Damage Fund got about $600 million (UN News). He proposed a "climate justice compact" for developing nations and warned the planet is heading for more than 2.5 degrees of warming even if current plans are fully carried out.

The US Justice Department charged three men over alleged Iran-backed plots, including one at RAF Fairford in England, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was moved to a military base after threats (New York Times). Iran's negotiator said the Strait of Hormuz, closed for over 250 days, "will never return" to its pre-war state (Al Jazeera).

In Spain, about four million workers joined the first general strike in 14 years, over housing costs, ahead of a snap election on 29 November (Reuters). Average rent of 1,176 euros a month is nearly a young worker's whole salary, Spain's Youth Council says.

Russia's ambassador to the United States, Alexander Darchiev, died suddenly in Moscow aged 66, the Russian Foreign Ministry said (Reuters). A spokeswoman later said he died of a chronic illness; envoy since March 2025.

Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient and began a 42-day countdown to being declared Ebola-free, expected on 23 December if no new case appears (Reuters). The Bundibugyo-strain outbreak caused nine cases, seven recoveries and two deaths.
